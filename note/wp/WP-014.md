---
id: WP-014
title: Monotonicity of rounding needs \round_double, not a double cast
kind: fact
path:
  - src/norm_2.c
related: [WP-001, WP-002, WP-007, PROOF-013]
---
Alt-Ergo proves a monotonicity step on a rounded value written `\round_double(\NearestEven, e)`
but not the same step written `(double)(e)`, so the assertions of `src/norm_2.c` that derive
`r >= m` from `r = m * t` and `t >= 1` use `\round_double`.

- WP's `Cfloat` theory maps `(double)(e)` to `to_f64`, whose axioms give the IEEE rounding only
  inside the range (`to_f64_range_round`) and infinities outside it; `\round_double(\NearestEven,
  e)` is Why3's `round RNE e`, for which the `ieee_float` theory has monotonicity and idempotence
  on representable values.
- In `src/norm_2.c`, after the finiteness test of `r`, the assertions are, in order: `t` finite,
  `s` finite, `t >= 1.0`, `m * t >= m` (exact), `r == \round_double(\NearestEven, m * t)`,
  `\round_double(\NearestEven, m) == m`, and the monotone step. With `(double)` casts the last
  two timed out; with `\round_double` all 90 goals prove. The postcondition
  `\abs(x[q]) <= *result` follows.
- The second loop's invariant `\ge_double(s, (double)1.0)` proves through the IEEE addition
  without help; it is what meets the `\gt_double(x, 0.0)` precondition of `diana_fp_sqrt`.
- The contract states only the order fact; the value of `r` is VST's (NORM-C5), as WP-001
  recommends for float values WP cannot prove.
